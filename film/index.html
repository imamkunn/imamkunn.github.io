Cinematic AI shorts · Imamkunn

Two short films, built shot by shot with AI

Each film started from a written script and a shot list, then went through storyboard, continuity-locked first frames, AI video, voice, sound design, score and a final film grade. Turn the sound on.

TWELVE PACES

Western · 66 s · dialogue

High noon. An old gunslinger and a young challenger, twelve paces apart, waiting for the clock tower bell. The duel turns personal before the shot.

What to watch for: a silent standoff built on master and over-the-shoulder coverage that respects the 180° line, one clock tower kept consistent across every angle, lip-synced 1880s-drawl dialogue, and a score that builds and cuts dead at the gunshot.

LAST LIGHT

Sci-fi · 55 s · dialogue

The last astronaut aboard Station Nusantara radios Earth, hoping someone is still listening.

What to watch for: one consistent character across interiors and exteriors, practical-looking cockpit lighting and quiet sound design carrying the isolation.

The workflow behind them

  1. Script & shot list

    Story, dialogue and a numbered shot list with a target length per shot, so generation time and credits go only where the edit needs them.

  2. Previz storyboard

    A quick 3D blocking pass in Blender to lock geography: who stands where, camera side, eyelines and the 180° line, before any image is made.

  3. Continuity-locked first frames

    Character and location reference sheets, then one first frame per shot (Nano Banana Pro). Every frame is checked against the master shot for faces, wardrobe, props and set pieces.

  4. Image-to-video

    Each frame animated in Google Flow (Veo 3.1) with one action per clip. Every clip is reviewed frame by frame at 4 fps; anything that breaks continuity or physics is regenerated.

  5. Voice & lip-sync

    Character voices with ElevenLabs. Where lips are on screen, the clip's own performance is converted into the character's voice, so the mouth movement stays in sync.

  6. Sound design & score

    Generated foley and ambience (wind, clocks, bells, gunshots) plus an original score, mixed so dialogue sits clearly above everything and the music ducks under it.

  7. Cut, pace & grade

    Tight cutting to the agreed tempo, a film grade that removes the over-sharp "AI look" (softening, halation, grain, gate weave, muted palette), 2.39 letterbox, subtitles and loudness normalised to −16 LUFS.
